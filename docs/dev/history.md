## Order crossover default

From "Order crossover" in `docs/dev/operators.md`.

A seed-paired release measurement on 2026-10-31 compared 0 with 0.3 over `fsm`, `fsm-timing`, `fsm-combined`, `mode-arbiter`, `takeoff`, `liquid-mixer`, `valu3s-uc6` and `fsm-lmcps`, seeds 1 to 3. At 0.3, repairs rose from 701 to 789. Runs with a repair implying an ideal fell from 9 to 8, and ideal-implying repairs fell from 40 to 32, mostly on `fsm`. Total wall time rose from 230 s to 249 s, with `liquid-mixer` and `valu3s-uc6` about 40% slower. Of 85 joins, 64 were proved and 15 scored realisable. Of 316 meets, only 69 were proved and 3 realisable, because a meet of two unrealisable parents seldom has every slot bounded. Of 163 relinkings, 62 bred a child and 18 scored realisable. Three seeds cannot separate these differences from noise.

## Grammar fixes

From "Grammar fixes" (formerly "Operator repairs of 2026-08-19") in `docs/dev/operators.md`. `experiments/2026-08-14-aurus-h2h/REPORT.md` measured the defects against 2,295 archived repairs.

The evidence is `experiments/2026-08-20-ops-grammar` and `experiments/2026-08-20-ops-weakening` (720 paired cases, `REPORT.md` in the latter). With the weakening screen off, as now ships, the pooled endpoint is null on both paths and the pre-registered rule said do not flip. The decision rests instead on reachability: with the screen on, `minepump` reads 0/20 under the legacy grammar against 12/20, while `humanoid-531`, which builds much larger formulae, loses runs to the repairs. Sweep O and the `ops-*`/`opswk-*` profiles are retired; those archives reproduce only through their vendored `scripts/`.

The constant folds in `src/prop_formula/simplify.cpp` exist because the `Constant` monotone rewrite left spellings such as `G(false)` standing, and 43.7% of formulae reaching `ltlsynt` mentioned a constant. They do not change wall time (paired A/B over 33 cases, sign p = 1.000). Both ops campaigns ran with them in both arms and attribute nothing to them.

A per-section bloat-cap baseline raised `arbiter-aurus`'s drop rate from 1.6% to 9.4% and cost 9 of its 10 ideal-implying runs, so the cap kept its specification-wide baseline.

## Assumption reach

From "Assumption construction" in `docs/dev/operators.md`.

The campaign `experiments/2026-08-26-assumption-reach` (sweep U) tested five keys. Its primary is null (exact McNemar p = 1.0000).

Five keys are removed. `[tlsf.mutation] max_assumption_width` (a body of up to n literals joined by `&` and `|`) and `p_bare_assumption` (an unconditional `F body` in place of `G F body`) each had one reach: `lift` repaired 3 of 12 under `reach-l`, and `lily11` rose from 5 to 11 of 12. Both stayed at their no-op defaults and were removed on 2026-10-30, so an appended body is one input literal and an unconditional assumption is always `G F`. `[tlsf.crossover] p_union_assumption` is unreachable by construction: `gyro-var2`'s ideal is a conjunct of `gyro-var1`'s specification and vice versa, material in a sibling no run sees, and arming it displaced clone-and-edit. `[tlsf.mutation] p_burst_continue` measured a loss (p = 0.0226), PEREDUR already mutating more aggressively than AuRUS's `Poisson(1)`; mutation applies exactly one edit. `[tlsf.mutation] p_remove_assumption` had no reach attributed. Sweep U and the `assumption-reach` profile are retired.

## Measured feature interactions

From "Measured feature interactions" in `docs/dev/operators.md`.

Two experiments measured how the six order-directed features interact (crossover, climb, demote, blame, gate and scale; parent-verdict pruning is left out because it cannot change a search result). Neither found a significant interaction of one feature with another. Neither should be repeated without a design that changes the power, and the reasons follow the results. Both measure ideal rate and found-repair rate on the nine small FRETISH specifications at seeds 0 to 29, with an exact Wilcoxon signed-rank test over (spec, seed) cells. Ideal rate counts only the seven specifications with a reachable ideal (210 cells); found-repair rate counts all nine (270 cells).

**Addition against removal.** The two-way ablation of `2026-11-05-fretish-order` gives each feature an *addition effect* (`only-f` less `base`) and a *removal effect* (`full` less `no-f`). With no interactions the two agree, and the addition effects sum to `full`'s gain over `base`. They sum to +20.0 points of ideal rate, against `full`'s +9.0 (p = 0.028 on the per-cell shortfall), so the gains overlap overall. No feature's removal effect exceeds its addition effect, and no single gap is significant: climb's is the largest, 4.3 points (p = 0.072), then blame's 3.8 (p = 0.19). This design cannot say which partners overlap, since every partner of a removed feature stays on in `full`.

**Pairs removed together.** `2026-11-16-fretish-order-pairs` (entry 075, commit `3d15f9b`, PR #236, tag `provenance/2026-11-16-fretish-order-pairs`) adds `no-a-b`, which is `full` with both features off, for the three pairs the code suggested would overlap: climb and gate share a step set, climb and demote both act on gutted repairs, and blame and scale act on the same mutation call. The interaction is (full − no-a-b) − (full − no-a) − (full − no-b), which is positive when the pair overlaps. `no-a` and `no-b` come from the first pass, at commits `f7ffb99` and `43b2fce`. The timing-bound specifications (`lift-plus-cruise-mini`, `valu3s-uc6`, `liquid-mixer`) behave differently across those commits, so their cells are read apart.

| Pair | Ideal, all | Ideal, timing-bound left out | Found-repair |
|---|---|---|---|
| climb + gate | +3.8 pp, p = 0.074 | +2.0 pp, p = 0.38 | 0.0 pp |
| climb + demote | −0.5 pp, p = 1.0 | −0.7 pp, p = 1.0 | 0.0 pp |
| blame + scale | −1.9 pp, p = 0.55 | −3.3 pp, p = 0.23 | −0.7 pp, p = 0.50 |

Climb and gate is the one pair that leans towards overlap, and most of its interaction sits on the timing-bound cells that cross commits. `no-blame-scale` finds no repair on `fsm-lmcps` in 30 runs, as `no-blame` and `no-scale` each do. `experiments/2026-11-16-fretish-order-pairs/PLAN.md` holds the full table, and `interactions.py` beside it recomputes it from the two result CSVs.

**Why not to repeat them.** At 30 seeds the largest effect in either experiment reaches only p = 0.07, so a null here does not show that two features are independent; a rerun at the same scale would return the same nulls. A real test needs several times the seeds or a corpus with more subjects. The seeded corpus (`2026-11-13-seeded-corpus`) answers the question that motivated the pairs: its `lean` arm, `full` without demote, scale, crossover and verdict, matches `full` on ideal rate (−2.2 pp, p = 0.17), so dropping those features together costs nothing measurable. Both experiments also measure only ideal and found-repair rates, and pooled repair strength is a different measure, which neither one tests. The paper carried both analyses in its evaluation (commits `f3fa112` and `0944277` in the paper repository) and dropped them in `2c9bf36` on 2026-11-16.

## VM testing

From "Testing" in `docs/vm.md`.

The disk was tested on 2026-09-11. It was booted under QEMU (Quick Emulator) with the Kernel-based Virtual Machine (KVM), on VirtualBox's default hardware: an AHCI disk, an e1000 network adapter and BIOS firmware. It was then checked over SSH on a forwarded port. The checks that passed:

- SSH answered 5 s after power-on.
- The tty1 autologin was active.
- `/etc/environment` supplied `PATH` and the `PEREDUR_*` variables to a non-interactive `ssh` command.
- `peredur --version` ran, and `realize $PEREDUR_EXAMPLES/lily02/spec.tlsf` printed `UNREALIZABLE`.
- A two-generation `peredur` run wrote `run.json`, and `scp` copied it out.
- `sudo` worked.
- The SSH host keys and `/etc/machine-id` were generated at first boot.
- The apt sources named the mirror.

`scripts/build_vm_image.sh`, run on that machine with `UBUNTU_MIRROR` set to the mirror above, took 1 min 55 s. The `.ova` it wrote is 171 MB and the `.vdi` 448 MB.

The appliance has not been booted in VirtualBox itself. The build machine runs Ubuntu 22.04.5 with kernel 6.8.0-138 and VirtualBox 6.1.50 from Ubuntu's packages. There VirtualBox intermittently halted VMs with *Guru Meditation* -2708 (`VERR_VMM_SET_JMP_ABORTED_RESUME`) within milliseconds of power-on, at the BIOS reset vector. A freshly created VM with no disk attached halted the same way. The fault is therefore in that host's VirtualBox, and says nothing about the appliance.

## Run manifest schema versions

From "Run manifest schema versions" in `docs/dev/config-and-provenance.md`. Versions 17 to 19 have no record.

- **2** added `fitness_cache`, `n_constant_folded` and `n_tautology_substitutions`, when those counters stopped printing to stdout by default and this became their only unconditional record. Anything `print_diagnostics_report` shows has to appear here too, or turning the flag off loses it.
- **3** added `input_screen`, so a campaign can partition its runs on whether the input itself failed a correctness check rather than grepping the log for the warning.
- **4** added `n_weak_operator_unresolved`. It counts satisfiability queries abandoned rather than answered, so a run that reports repairs with a non-zero count screened less than a run reporting the same repairs with zero — which is not visible from any other field.
- **5** added `budget_screen`, so a zero-yield run can be told apart from one whose ltlsynt budget could not decide its own input specification.
- **6** added `fitness.status_grading`, since the status objective now has two scales and a manifest that does not name the one in use cannot be read.
- **7** dropped `fitness.weight_halstead` along with the objective it weighted, so the objective vector is one shorter and a reader keying on its position against an older manifest reads the wrong component.
- **8** added `mutation.p_remove_guarantee`. The operator can delete a guarantee, so a repair may hold fewer requirements than the specification it came from, and a reader comparing counts needs to know whether the run could do that.
- **9** added `n_spot_decided` and `n_escalations_declined`, and redefined `tool_calls.black.calls` as black's own exec count rather than the satisfiability cache-miss count, which SPOT taking the first stage split in two. Comparing `black.calls` across the boundary compares different things.
- **10** added `n_well_separation_errors`. The well-separation query used to let an ltlsynt error end the run, so a run that finished implies zero of them; from this version a finished run may have dropped candidates on a non-answer, and only this field says how many.
- **11** added `input_screen_error`. Before it, a screen that raised ended the run before any manifest was written, so `input_screen: null` meant the input passed every check. From this version the run survives, and null means either that or a screen that never produced a verdict — this field is what tells them apart.
- **12** added `fitness.mrs_admission_order`. The MRS walk's score depends on the order it admits parts in, so two runs on one specification under one grading scale are not comparable without it.
- **13** added `genetic.accumulate_repairs` and `n_accumulated_repairs`. Before it a run's repairs all came from its final population, so the output was a function of the last generation alone; from this version the key can union in the repairs earlier generations found, and only this field says how many of them the run would otherwise have thrown away.
- **14** added `genetic.repaired_operators`. It selected between two mutation and crossover grammars with different RNG draw streams, so two runs of the same seed agreed only when they agreed on this key.
- **15** removed `genetic.repaired_operators`. The repaired grammar is now the only one, so the key no longer selects anything; a manifest at this version or later was written by a binary that has only that grammar.
- **16** added `n_ltlsynt_capability_errors`. Before it, an ltlsynt run that reported a limit of its own instead of a verdict ended the run and no manifest was written at all; from this version the query resolves as undecided and this field is the only record that it happened.
- **20** redefined `tool_calls.ltlfilt.calls` and added the caches block. `calls` counted `simplify_ltl`'s and `rewrite_weak_operators`' cache misses, while `total_s` beside it also covered every `spot_satisfiable` exec, which is memoised nowhere — so the field understated ltlfilt's true exec count by 35.5% over a 14-specification corpus and the seconds per call derived from the pair were over two different sets. It is now the exec count `total_s` is actually over. `caches` reports the hits and misses of every memo in the run, which no field carried before: the satisfiability cache's miss count reached no field at all (the black row reports black's own exec count, deliberately, since SPOT takes the first stage), and it is the largest cache in a run at 237,969 lookups over that corpus.
- **21** added `stopped_by`, `generations_run` and `individuals_bred`, with `genetic.termination`, `genetic.max_individuals` and `genetic.max_wall_s` beside them in the config block. A run could end only by exhausting `genetic.generations` before this version, so its wall time and its generation count said everything there was to say about how it stopped; from this version it can also spend an offspring budget or pass a deadline, and only `stopped_by` separates the three. `individuals_bred` is null wherever no budget was active, an unbudgeted run not paying for the count.
- **22** added `mutation.p_condition_type` and `mutation.p_scope`, the two directional arms over a requirement's scope and condition type.
- **23** added the implication block. The pairwise sweep's counters reached no field at all before it, so a run's diagnostics carried them and a campaign reading `run.json` could not: `n_equivalent_collapsed` in particular is the only record of how much of the output was one repair written twice, and it is unreadable without `n_comparisons` beside it to say how many pairs were asked.
- **24** added "aurus" to the values `fitness.status_grading` can take. It exists to be ablated against rather than to be run: it reproduces AuRUS's own ladder so a campaign can cross PEREDUR's grading against the design PEREDUR derives from, and it is the one scale that does not cap a candidate gaming its own assumptions. A reader keying on that field against an older manifest can assume it names one of two scales that do; from this version it may name a third that does not, so the status objective of two runs is comparable only where the field agrees.
- **25** added `mutation.p_monotone`, the FRETISH monotone rewrite arm. Its TLSF twin `tlsf.mutation.p_monotone` has been in the config block since the block existed, so a reader keying on the name alone now finds two.
- **26** added `implication.fingerprint_refuted`, the ordered pairs a sampled word settled ahead of the solver. It is the only reading of whether the prefilter worked on a given run, the pairs it refutes never reaching `comparisons`. The same version is where the FRETISH implication check stopped decomposing per requirement, so `comparisons`, `timeouts` and the repair counts beside them are not comparable across it on that path.
- **27** removed `tlsf.mutation.p_monotone` (folded into `mutation.p_monotone`, which both paths now read), `tlsf.mutation.connective_implies` and the two `tlsf.mutation.monotone_*_rules` gates (all now unconditional), and `filters.run_weakening` and `filters.run_well_separation` (the stages are gone). It also removed `filters.run_vacuity` (vacuity always runs per generation), `mutation.allow_output_assumptions` (always allowed), `tlsf.mutation.p_remove_assumption` and `p_burst_continue` (their operators are gone), `runtime.ganak_timeout_ms` (ganak runs untimed) and `runtime.max_concurrent_realizability` (no ltlsynt concurrency cap), and dropped `tool_calls.ganak.timeouts` with the timeout it counted. The same version moved the fitness weights to AuRUS's 0.1/0.2/0.7 and `mutation.p_condition_type`, `p_scope` and `p_monotone` off 0.
- **28** moved both paths onto one implication sweep and runs it during the search. On TLSF, `comparisons`, `skipped` and `duplicates` read 0 on every earlier manifest, the TLSF sweep having counted none of them, and `fingerprint_refuted` was never reset; all four now carry the sweep's own figures, as they already did on FRETISH. `timeouts` still reads 0 there, `tlsf_spec_implies` counting none. Wherever the run accumulates, the implication figures are the streamed sweep's, whose batches ask the pairs within a batch and between it and the maximal set so far, so `comparisons` and `skipped` do not compare with any earlier run's; `duplicates` reads 0, the stream deduplicating before its sweep sees anything. No key selects between the two routes, so a manifest at 28 or above carries the streamed figures whenever `accumulate_repairs` and `filters.run_implication` are both on.
- **29** added `mutation.p_stop`, the FRETISH arm rewriting the stop condition of an `until` or `before` timing. It draws nothing on a specification without one, so an earlier run's search is what it would have been at any value.
- **30** counts `implication.timeouts` on TLSF, where `tlsf_spec_implies` counted none and the field read 0 on every earlier TLSF manifest.
- **31** records the config keys a path never reads as null on that path: the FRETISH-only [mutation] rewrite arms on a TLSF run, and [tlsf] `muc_max_iterations` and [`tlsf.mutation`] on a FRETISH run. An earlier manifest records whatever the config said, which the search ignored.
- **32** added `fitness.keyword_similarity` and `fitness.semantic_trace_weight`, which place the FRETISH timing, scope and condition-type measures in the syntactic or the semantic objective. The default reproduces every earlier run, so an older manifest reads as `keyword_similarity` = "syntactic". Both are FRETISH-only and read null on a TLSF run.
- **33** added `mrs_screen`: how often the MRS walk's whole-specification satisfiability screen ran, fired and was left undecided. Zero under any other status grading, and absent before the screen existed.
- **34** added `n_model_probe_sat`: implication queries black answered SAT between SPOT's first look and its full budget. `tool_calls.black.calls` now counts those probes too, so an implication-heavy run reads far more black calls than one at 33, and `n_escalations_declined` still counts only the queries left undecided.
- **35** added `implication.residual_queries` and `implication.residual_settled`: the implication checks that dropped shared guarantees from the negated side, and those settled true with nothing left to ask.
- **36** added `implication.rule_covered`: the requirements a structural rule proved implied while building that shortened destination.
- **37** added `caches.realizability.rule_subsumed`: the realizability queries settled from a decided one by ordering the requirements they replaced.
- **38** added `caches.realizability.satisfiability_routed`: the realizability queries over no environment inputs decided by satisfiability rather than by ltlsynt. `tool_calls.ltlsynt.calls` no longer counts them, so a run on such a specification reads far fewer ltlsynt calls than one at 37.
- **39** added `implication.model_words` and `implication.model_refuted`: the solver models the prefilter adds to its sampled words, and the ordered pairs only a model word refuted. From this version `fingerprint_refuted` counts those pairs too, so it reads higher than at 38 wherever a model separated a pair.
- **40** added `caches.realizability.controller_reuse`: the realizability queries put to a stored controller, those it answered, and the guarantee checks that cost; and `tool_calls.controller_check`, the ltl2tgba and autfilt execs behind them. `tool_calls.ltlsynt.calls` falls by the hits.
- **41** added `runtime.mrs_speculation_depth` and `mrs_speculation`: the MRS walk's lookahead queries issued, started and later asked by the walk itself. The lookahead moves no score, but its untaken branches add ltlsynt execs, so `tool_calls.ltlsynt.calls` reads higher than at 40 at the default depth.
- **42** removed `caches.simplify_ltl` with the `simplify_ltl` pass, which no engine path had called for many versions; its row read zero hits and misses. `tool_calls.ltlfilt.cache_hits`, which counted that pass's hits and so read zero too, now counts the `remove_wm` memo's hits.
- **43** removed `tlsf.mutation.max_assumption_width` and `p_bare_assumption`, retired at their no-op defaults.
- **44** removed `tlsf.repair_mode` and `tlsf.muc_max_iterations` with the MUC-guided repair mode; every TLSF run is monolithic.
- **45** removed `fitness.keyword_similarity` and `fitness.semantic_trace_weight`; the FRETISH keyword orders always join the semantic objective at weight 1/2.
- **46** removed `genetic.accumulate_repairs`; every run accumulates.
- **47** removed `model_counting.metric`; trace counts always combine as logarithms.
- **48** removed `runtime.dashboard` with the live dashboard and its `progress.jsonl` stream.
- **49** added `mrs_speculation.killed`: lookahead queries stopped once the walk could no longer read them, their exec killed rather than run to the ltlsynt timeout. `tool_calls.ltlsynt` counts no killed exec, so its calls and timeouts read lower than at 48.
- **50** added `runtime.mrs_lookahead_kill_grace_ms`: how long an abandoned lookahead query may run before it is killed. At the default of 2000, `mrs_speculation.killed` counts only queries past the grace, so it reads lower than at 49.
- **51** added `mutation.blame_targeted_slots` and `n_targeted_mutations`, the count of rewrites whose slot that key drew from a narrowed list. The count reads 0 with the key off, which is the default.
- **52** added `config.genetic.maximality_demotion` and `n_maximality_demotions`: the candidates moved one front down at survivor selection as proved weaker than another realizable one (see "Maximality demotion" in `docs/dev/algorithm.md`). It is 0 wherever the key is off, and the key reads null on a TLSF run.
- **53** added `genetic.p_order_crossover` and the `order_crossover` object, which holds `attempted`, `proved` and `realizable` counts under `join`, `meet` and `relink`. The key defaults to 0, under which every count is 0, and a TLSF run records the key as null.
- **54** added `config.filters.climb_output`, `climb_order` and `climb_orders`, and the `climb` block (`climbed`, `steps_tried`, `steps_accepted`, `queries`, `ltlsynt_execs`), which is all zero with the climb off. FRETISH only, so a TLSF run records the three keys as null.
- **55** added `mutation.direction_gated`, `mutation.p_sideways` and the `direction_gate` object: `forbidden` and `sideways_refused` count the free rewrites returned to the parent, and `steps` the realizable parents' steps back towards the original. All three read 0 with the gate off, which is the default.
- **56** added `filters.parent_verdict` and the `parent_verdict` object: `dropped` counts the offspring the parent-verdict stage removed as proved strengthenings of an unrealizable parent, and `unproved` the proved strengthenings it kept because the parent's verdict was undecided. Both read 0 with the key off, which is the default.
- **57** added `mutation.step_scaling`, `mutation.bisect_steps`, `direction_gate.bisected` and the `step_scaling` object. `bisected` counts the steps towards the original that moved to the midpoint of their timing chain. `scaled` counts the mutations run under a step scale, `large` the large moves they took (a removed guarantee, a timing moved off Always or Eventually, a tick-count jump) and `small` the tick-count steps that took a single edge. All read 0 with both keys off, which is the default, and a TLSF run records the two keys as null.
- **58** added `memo_hits`, `subsumed`, `rule_subsumed`, `satisfiability_routed` and `controller_hits` to the `climb` block. With `ltlsynt_execs` they split every realizability query the climb's gate asked by what answered it, and each query lands in exactly one; `queries` counts gate candidates, each of which asks several. All read 0 with the climb off.
- **59** added the `caches.realizability.execs` object, splitting the ltlsynt execs (`misses`) by outcome: `realizable`, `unrealizable`, `timed_out` and `capability_errors`. Within them it also counts `speculative`, the execs the MRS walk's lookahead asked that returned, and `decided_over_1s`, `_2s` and `_5s` with `slowest_decided_s`, the decided execs a lower `ltlsynt_timeout_ms` would have left undecided. It also records the new `[runtime] controller_reuse_min_guarantees` key in `config`.
- **60** added `n_spot_sat_refuted`, `n_spot_sat_unconfirmed` and the `tool_calls.spot_emptiness` row. A SAT from SPOT is now confirmed by black, so `n_spot_decided` counts SPOT's UNSAT answers and its confirmed SAT ones, and `tool_calls.black.calls` gains one exec per SPOT SAT. A run at 60 reads far more black calls than one at 59. See "Satisfiability route" in `docs/dev/performance.md`.
- **61** added `decompose_reasks` and `reasks_decided` to `caches.realizability.execs`, and records the new `[runtime] ltlsynt_decompose_budget_ms` key in `config`. A re-asked query is two ltlsynt execs, counted once in `misses` and by the second's outcome, and its time in `tool_calls.ltlsynt` is both attempts'. See "Decomposition re-ask" in `docs/dev/performance.md`.
- **62** added `gated` to `caches.realizability.controller_reuse`, the queries the store would have taken but for the screen-time gate, and records the new `[runtime] controller_reuse_min_screen_ms` key in `config`. A run whose screened input query took under 40 ms now reads zero `attempts`, `hits` and `stored`, and more `misses`. See "Controller-store gate" in `docs/dev/performance.md`.
